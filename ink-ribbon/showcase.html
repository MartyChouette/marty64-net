Ink Ribbon

Two rival families' typists fall in love through hidden messages buried in official correspondence, and their secret language becomes the key to preventing a war.

Ink Ribbon is a narrative typing puzzle game. You play a family-employed typist in 1914, in a small Rovellan town on the edge of war. You type the daily correspondence from inside and outside the estate, and you begin to pick up suspicious patterns inside the letters. From small gossip to the tides of war, it is a game about paying attention and corresponding in codes.

The setting

Rovella is a small border town built on ink and paper mills, in the contested region of Trentara where three nations meet. The Aurine Empire, the Serenna Republic, and the Helvetar Cantons. Two families wield immense power over the Rovellan locals. The Epaule hold the mayorship and support the sovereign Serennan South. The rival Aguien hold the ambassadorship and answer to the growing Aurine Empire, contesting from the North. One family owns the town. The other outranks it.

The core design

You type what you are told. But what is encoded is the game.

You are a typist for the Epaule family, working from a desk in the estate study. Across the street, another typist works for the rival Aguien household. You never meet. Your letters cross only through the documents the two houses exchange each day.

At first you notice anomalies in the documents from across the street. An extra space. An odd word choice. These are not mistakes. Someone on the other side is trying to say something.

Over the unfolding days, a romance grows in those secrets and symbols. You uncover more messages and work to put out the tensions and family dramas the coming war brings.

Ink Ribbon is a game about how encoding information isn't about hiding it. It's about channeling it.

The secret language

Ciphers in three channels

The language is never handed to you. It is always present, you just cannot see it yet. Routine-looking early documents were carrying messages you could not read at the time. The game trains a new way of reading until you cannot look at a typed page without searching it.

Typewriter ciphers hide in the typed text itself, readable right at the desk. Candlelight ciphers need a flame. Physical ciphers need you to do something to the paper. Pick one and reveal it.

Acrostic

TypewriterInkBasic

The first letter of each line spells a hidden message. Read down the left margin.

Real technique: the acrostic null cipher. First-letter messages hide in Renaissance dedications, and the form was used to smuggle text past censors who read every word but never read the margin.

◈ AGUIEN ◈
Office of the Imperial Ambassador
Have the remaining census forms been collected from the ward offices?
Each district must file independently before the quarterly deadline.
Late submissions will not be accepted under the current protocol.
Please direct any questions regarding procedure to the chief clerk.
Updated records should reflect the November amendments without exception.
Standard filing procedures apply to all remaining departments.
Hidden Message
HELP US

Ink Flutter

TypewriterInkBasic

An extra space makes the carriage advance without striking. The ribbon drags and leaves a faint smear. Two flutter marks bracket the hidden segment. The words between them are the message.

Real technique: whitespace steganography. The meaning rides in the spacing rather than the letters. Modern tools hide data in the gaps between words the same way.

◈ AGUIEN ◈
Office of the Imperial Ambassador
Delivery schedules for the western district remain unchanged.

routes will continue for all official correspondence.
No further action is required at this time.
Hidden Message
"they are opening the western mail"

The surface still parses: "We confirm the standard routes will continue..." The flutter marks cause a slight pause and ink disturbance. The words between them are the payload.

Carriage Return Stripes

TypewriterInkIntermediate

Returning the carriage with the ribbon engaged drags a faint line across the full width of the page. Some lines have it, some do not. The stripe is always visible. The cipher is the pattern.

Real technique: the grille cipher (Cardano, 1550). A grille or marker designates which lines carry the message while the rest of the page stays as camouflage.

Example A: Supply Requisition → Safe Combination
◈ AGUIEN ◈
Office of the Imperial Ambassador
Please deliver 4 crates of standard filing paper.
The office requires 17 new ink ribbons this quarter.
We need 8 replacement ledgers for the accounting desk.
Additional 12 candles should be included if available.
Send 32 boxes of correspondence envelopes, cream stock.
Standard sealing wax, 6 sticks in the usual red.
We also need 2 bottles of ink, Rovella blend.
Include 5 reams of carbon paper for duplicates.
Hidden: Safe Combination
17 - 32 - 5

Eight lines, eight quantities. The stripes pick out three: 17, 32, 5. A combination lock. The other five numbers are real supply requests. Without the stripes you are staring at a grocery list.

A letter typed in uppercase when it should be lowercase. The unexpected capitals spell a hidden word.

Real technique: a null cipher keyed to typography. The reader ignores the words and collects only the letters that break the rule, here the misplaced capitals.

Twisted Path

TypewriterInkAdvanced

The hidden message rides inside text that reads normally on its own. Every Nth word carries it. A margin symbol tells the reader the interval.

Real technique: the every-Nth-word null cipher, a spy-craft staple. The carrier text reads as ordinary correspondence while the message sits on a fixed interval.

◈ AGUIEN ◈
Office of the Imperial Ambassador
···
The current orders are all arriving by mail tomorrow
before the morning session begins. Sincerely,
the office of the ambassador.
Hidden Message (every 3rd word)
Orders arriving tomorrow morning.

The surface reads as normal correspondence. Three dots in the margin tell the reader: every 3rd word. The message hides in plain sight because it is part of the sentence.

Red Keywords

TypewriterDictationEditorial

During dictation the speaker emphasizes hostile words. If you type them, they appear red on the page. A page full of red faithfully transmitted the speaker's hostility. A page with no red is one where you softened every edge. This is editorial control made visible.

Real parallel: the latitude of the diplomatic translator. Faithful transmission versus softened phrasing is a real and consequential choice in treaty and dispatch work.

◈ EPAULE ◈
Office of the Mayor · Rovella, Trentara
Dictation (spoken by the Matriarch):
"We CATEGORICALLY REFUSE to entertain any further DEMANDS regarding the mill. This position is FINAL and NON-NEGOTIABLE."
Typed faithfully:
We categorically refuse to entertain any further
demands regarding the mill. This position is final
and non-negotiable.
Typed with editorial control:
We decline to discuss the mill matter further at this time.
The Point
The red words are the hostile ones from the dictation, transmitting the Matriarch's anger at full force. The edited version below has no red because you softened every word. Same content, different temperature. You decide which letter the ambassador reads.

Sympathetic Ink

CandlelightHeatIntermediate

Written between the typed lines in lemon juice or milk. Invisible when dry. Warm the paper near the candle and the organic compounds oxidize brown. The reveal is permanent. Once browned, it stays.

Real technique: sympathetic ink. Organic fluids like lemon juice or milk vanish when dry and brown under heat. Washington's Culper spy ring relied on it during the American Revolution.

◈ AGUIEN ◈
Office of the Imperial Ambassador
The imperial administration has completed its review.
the ambassador is afraid
All departments have reported satisfactory results.
he wants to refuse the order
Standard procedures will continue as outlined.
give him a reason on paper
No further action is required at this time.
Hidden Message (between the lines)
The ambassador is afraid. He wants to refuse the order. Give him a reason on paper.

Three lines of hidden text layered into a routine document. The typed text is the envelope. The sympathetic ink is the letter.

Pin Pricks

CandlelightBacklitBasic

One tiny hole punched through the paper per line, each at a specific horizontal position. Invisible in daylight. Place the candle behind the page and each hole becomes a point of warm light. The positions encode letters.

Real technique: the pin-prick cipher. Tiny holes pricked above letters spell a message when the page is held to light. Used by lovers, prisoners, and Victorians dodging postage.

◈ AGUIEN ◈
Office of the Imperial Ambassador
The quarterly reports have been compiled and submitted.
Every district office has confirmed its annual filings.
No discrepancies were found in the current period.
All records are available for inspection upon request.
Municipal archives will remain open during regular hours.
Please direct further inquiries to the chief clerk.
Decoder stick · lay it across the page: each hole falls under one letter
ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ
Hidden Message
D - A - N - G - E - R

Six points of light, one per line. The decoder stick is a strip marked with the alphabet, evenly spaced. Lay it across the page and each hole falls under one letter. Read top to bottom: D, A, N, G, E, R. Without the stick you see only golden dots where randomness should be. The spacing is the key.

Crease Codes

PhysicalFoldAdvanced

The letter arrives folded in half, but there is a second crease at an unusual position. Refold along the odd crease and read down: the characters at that column spell the hidden message.

Real technique: a fold-based grille. Refolding the sheet aligns a hidden column, the same selection principle behind the Cardan grille applied through paper geometry.

◈ AGUIEN ◈
Office of the Imperial Ambassador
The formal review confirms all records are in order.
Deliveries must be confirmed before the deadline.
The hall archives remain available for inspection.
Controlled access to the east wing begins Monday.
MILL
Hidden Message (read down the odd fold)
M - I - L - L

The letter arrives folded in half. Normal. But there is a second crease at an unusual position. Refold along it and read the characters at that column: "MILL." The paper mill. The thing the families are fighting over.

Water Reveal

PhysicalWaterBasic

Written with a solution invisible when dry. A water glass sits at the edge of the desk. Its condensation drips onto the corner of the document, the moisture seeps in, and hidden text blooms where it spreads. The glass has been there since Day 1.

Real technique: invisible ink developed by a reagent. Some sympathetic inks stay hidden until moisture or a chemical wash brings them up, exactly as the condensation does here.

Three lines of invisible ink seeping into view as the paper darkens. The glass on the desk was never in the way. It was the tool. You did not spill water on a document. The document was waiting to be spilled on.

The desk · the level

The whole game happens at one desk

The typewriter fills the foreground. A window sits directly ahead, looking onto the street and across to the Aguien residence. The typewriter tells you what is happening in words. The window shows you what is happening in the world. When they contradict, when the letter says peace but the street says war, that is the tension.

The job is dictation. A family member or official reads correspondence aloud and you type what you hear. The audio is crackling and mechanical, 1914-period, and the imperfection gives you cover for the words you miss on purpose.

One chair, three directions. The camera pans left, forward, and right, and never changes position. Most of the game is spent looking forward at the page.

Floor plan · the player never leaves the chair
What you see · look left, forward, right
Look left
The Window
Observation. The street and the Aguien residence across it. No interaction, pure reading. This is the scoreboard.
Look forward
The Desk
Gameplay. The typewriter, the incoming stack, the page. All typing, encoding, and editorial control happens here.
Look right
The Shelf
Study. The codebook that grows as you decode, the saved correspondence, the trade-cipher references. Your knowledge made physical.

Every page you type has three layers working at once.

Accuracy
Can you type it correctly? Errors draw scrutiny. Too many and you are dismissed, which is game over. You lose your seat at the typewriter and your only connection to the other side.
Editorial control
You are transcribing speech, so you control the written record. Drop a line of troop numbers and it never reaches the recipient. Change "demand" to "request" and the ambassador reads a calmer letter than the one that was spoken. "I didn't hear that part clearly, sir." Mistakes are expected. Too many convenient ones and the family gets suspicious.
Secret language
The other typist's coded words arrive embedded in the documents being read aloud. You have to hear the code while typing the surface text. Dual attention. Performing your job and holding a private conversation in the same keystrokes.

The street window

You are never told your score. You look up and see it.

Your whole sense of the outside world comes through two channels: the documents on the desk and the street through the window. Every element in the window is an indicator, none of them decorative. Each tracks the town from peace to war and responds to your choices with a delay. A change today shows up days later.

Sometimes the two channels agree. Sometimes they contradict. You read an incoming letter that says all matters are proceeding normally, you look up, and there is a flag on the Aguien residence that was not there yesterday.
