SNOOP

A 1-bit detective game for Playdate

50 Buildings | 8 Districts | 25 Clues | 4300 Lines of C++ | Zero Assets

The Game

Snoop is a detective game about breaking into buildings, searching through drawers and filing cabinets, and uncovering a conspiracy that runs through an entire city. You play as a private investigator working alone. Nobody asked you to do this. The evidence started showing up and you followed it.

Every room uses a 4-wall mechanic. You face a desk, a window, a door, or a closet, and rotate between them with the D-pad. Each wall has its own interactive elements. The crank opens drawers, turns locks, and adjusts dials. Everything you find goes onto a corkboard where connections between clues reveal the larger picture.

Buildings have visitors. They arrive on timers, walk through the space, and can catch you if you're standing in the wrong place. Different visitor types behave differently. Some patrol. Some check specific rooms. Some turn around at random. You listen for footsteps, hide in closets, and wait them out.

The story follows a 4-act structure. You start with isolated break-ins and disconnected clues. By the final act you're inside The Architect's penthouse, piecing together a city-wide operation. There are no sprites and no imported images. Every visual in the game is drawn procedurally from lines, rectangles, and ellipses. The entire game is a single C++ source file.

Technical

Platform

Playdate handheld. 400x240 screen, 1-bit display. Black and white only, no grayscale. Dither patterns used for all shading.

Language

C++17, compiled with MinGW. The entire game lives in a single source file. No engine, no framework, direct Playdate C API.

Graphics

All procedural draw calls. 299 total: 187 drawLine, 76 fillRect, plus ellipses and rects. No sprites, no bitmaps, no imported assets of any kind.

Audio

Two synth channels with 9 generated sound effects. Footsteps, drawer slides, lock clicks, paper rustling. All synthesized at runtime.

Memory

Zero dynamic allocation. All game state uses static arrays sized at compile time. No malloc, no new, no heap fragmentation.

Build

CMake + MinGW toolchain. Compiles to Snoop.pdx for the Playdate Simulator and hardware device.

Visitor AI

Four visitor types patrol the buildings. Each type has different movement patterns, threat levels, and countermeasures. When a visitor turns around unexpectedly, the game pulls from a pool of flavor text lines to describe why, keeping the tension unpredictable.

Type Threat Behavior Counter Turnaround
Security Guard High Fixed patrol route through all rooms. Checks corners and closets. Time the patrol loop. Hide before they reach your room. 30%
Office Worker Medium Goes to a specific room, stays there, then leaves. Predictable path. Avoid their destination room. Wait for them to settle. 15%
Janitor Medium Wanders between rooms at random. No fixed route. Slow movement. Listen for footsteps. Move when they move away. 40%
Executive Low Walks directly to their office and stays. Rarely leaves. Stay out of the executive office. Search other rooms freely. 10%

Turnaround chance is rolled each time a visitor reaches a waypoint. When triggered, a random flavor text line plays to explain the reversal.

Evidence System

25 Key Clues
15 Connections
4 Acts

Clues are hidden inside drawers, filing cabinets, safes, and desk compartments across the city's 50 buildings. Each clue is a physical object: a memo, a photograph, a shipping receipt, a phone number scrawled on a napkin. When you find one, it goes onto the corkboard.

The corkboard is the evidence board. Clues are pinned to it, and connections between them are drawn as string links. Some connections unlock automatically when you have both clues. Others require you to place them next to each other and confirm the link. Each connection advances the story.

The conspiracy builds across 4 acts. Act 1 is isolated cases with no obvious pattern. Act 2 reveals shared names and addresses. Act 3 exposes the network. Act 4 takes you to The Architect's penthouse where the full scope of the city-wide operation becomes clear.
